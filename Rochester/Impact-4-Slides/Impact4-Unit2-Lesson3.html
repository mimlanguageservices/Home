Future tenses
Describing events in the future
Future simple
will + bare infinitive
We will help you put this village on a map.
Future continuous
will + be + verb ending in -ing
What will you be doing at eight o’clock tomorrow?
Future perfect
will + have + past participle

Grammar Explanation Part A
Future simple: uses
We use the future simple (will + bare infinitive):
1
to make predictions.
Over the next few years, people will look online to find volunteers to help with disaster relief.
2
to communicate spontaneous decisions.
I’ll go to the meeting this evening.
3
to talk about future facts.
The global population will continue to grow in the twenty-first century.
4
to make promises.
We will help you put this village on a map.
5
for questions and requests.
Will you join YouthMappers with us?
Grammar Explanation Part A
Future simple for deadlines
Promise it, decide it, predict it.
Form
will + verb
✓ I will (I’ll) send it.
✗ I will not (won’t) send it.
? Will you send it?
Use
We use the future simple to promise or decide something about meeting a deadline, or to predict whether it will be on time.
Time words: tomorrow · tonight · next week · on Friday · soon
Examples
I will send the report tomorrow.
We won’t miss the deadline.
Will you finish it by Friday?
Timeline Part A
Future simple on a timeline
We decide or promise now, and the action happens later.
will + verbI will send the report tomorrow.

Grammar Explanation Part A
Future continuous: uses
We use the future continuous (will + be + verb ending in -ing):
1
to talk about the duration of a future action.
I’ll be working in Mexico for a year.
2
for an action that is in progress at a specific time in the future.
What will you be doing at eight o’clock tomorrow?
Grammar Explanation Part A
Future continuous for deadlines
What will you be busy doing?
Form
will be + verb-ing
✓ I will be working.
✗ I won’t be working.
? Will you be working?
Use
We use the future continuous for an action that will be in progress at a specific time, often to show how busy we will be before a deadline.
Time words: at 8 p.m. · this time tomorrow · all day · from 4 to 6
Examples
At 8 p.m. tonight I will be finishing my essay.
This time tomorrow we will be presenting.
Will you be working all weekend?
Timeline Part A
Future continuous on a timeline
At one moment in the future, the action is already happening.
will be + verb-ingAt 8 p.m. tonight I will be working on my essay.

Grammar Explanation Part A
Future perfect: use
We use the future perfect (will + have + past participle):
1
for an action that will be complete by a specific time in the future, or before another future action.
By the time I’m 50, scientists will have discovered many new ways to help humanity.
Grammar Explanation Part A
Future perfect for deadlines
Finished before the deadline.
Form
will have + past participle
✓ I will have finished.
✗ I won’t have finished.
? Will you have finished?
Use
We use the future perfect for an action that will be completed before a deadline. It looks back from a point in the future.
Time words: by · by the time · before · by then
Examples
By Friday I will have finished the project.
We will have submitted it by noon.
Will you have finished by Friday?
Timeline Part A
Future perfect on a timeline
We look back from the deadline: the action is already complete.
will have + past participleBy Friday I will have finished the project.
