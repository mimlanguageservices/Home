Grammar Explanation Part A
Talking about amounts
We use percentages, fractions and quantifiers to say how many:
1
Half of / Fifty percent of  =  50%
Fifty percent of the world’s population is under thirty.
2
Over half of  =  more than 50%
Over half of my classmates live close to the school.
3
Two-thirds of  =  about 67%  ·  Three-quarters of  =  75%
Around two-thirds of people have a smartphone. (68%)
4
The majority of / Most of  =  more than half
The majority of people listen to the radio every week.
5
A lot of / A number of  =  many (we don’t say exactly)
A lot of people watch videos on their phones.
Grammar Explanation Part A
Percentages and fractions
Say how big the group is.
Form
quantifier + of + noun
✓ two-thirds of the students
✓ fifty percent of people
✗ fifty percents · two-third
Use
We use quantifiers to report results and statistics: how many people in a group do something.
Useful words: around · about · almost · nearly · just over
Examples
Half of the class walks to school.
Around two-thirds of people have a smartphone.
Most of my friends play video games.
Scale Part A
From none to all
Every quantifier has a place on the line.
quantifier + of + nounAround two-thirds of people have a smartphone.

Grammar Explanation Part A
Verb agreement: the rule
The verb agrees with the noun after the quantifier, not with the quantifier:
1
uncountable or singular group noun → singular verb
Fifty percent of the world’s population is under thirty.
The majority of the population has a TV set.
2
plural countable noun → plural verb
Sixty-eight percent of people have a smartphone.
The majority of people listen to the radio every week.
Grammar Explanation Part A
Subject-verb agreement
Look past the quantifier. Find the noun.
Form
quantifier + of + noun + verb
singular / uncountable noun → is, has, lives
plural noun → are, have, live
Use
The quantifier tells us how much. The noun tells us what, and the noun chooses the verb.
Always singular: the population · the news · the water · the money
Examples
Most of the news is online.
Most of my friends are online.
Half of the pizza is gone. Half of the pizzas are gone.

Grammar Explanation Part A
A number of or the number of?
One small word changes the meaning and the verb:
1
a number of = several / many → plural verb
A number of radio stations are online.
2
the number of = the total, one figure → singular verb
The number of radio stations worldwide is 44,000.
Grammar Explanation Part A
A number of / the number of
Many things, or one total?
Form
a number of + plural noun + plural verb
the number of + plural noun + singular verb
Use
Use a number of when there are many and you don’t say exactly how many. Use the number of to give the total.
The number of ... is growing · is falling · is 28
Examples
A number of students speak three languages.
The number of students in our class is 28.
The number of phone users grows every year.
Diagram Part A
Many things or one total?
A number of = many. The number of = one figure.
a number of → are  ·  the number of → isThe number of radio stations is 44,000.
