Choose Your Story Part A
What story will your map tell?
A👥

Where do people live?

Population and settlement
Show density, distribution or major cities.

Example: shade municipalities low / medium / high.

B🌡️

Hot, cold, or in between?

Climate and geography
Show temperature, rainfall, elevation or ecosystems.

Example: a color gradient for temperature.

C⛏️

What lies beneath the land?

Natural resources
Show minerals, coal, gold, oil or other resources.

Example: symbols for deposits, production sites and mining.

D🤝

Who are the people of this land?

Indigenous communities and cultural geography
Show officially documented territories, reservations and resguardos.

Use respectful words and reliable sources.

E🌿

Nature worth protecting

Environment and biodiversity
Show national parks, protected areas, rivers or forests.

Example: shaded areas for protected zones.

F🌾

What does this department produce?

Agriculture and the economy
Show crops, livestock or other major activities.

Example: symbols for coffee, rice, cacao or bananas.

The Map Checklist Part A
What every map must include
0 / 8
✓
1. Department outline
Show the department's approximate boundaries and identify it by name.
✓
2. Clear title
Give the map a specific title, such as “Population Density in Antioquia.”
✓
3. Thematic display
Show the chosen information with colors, symbols, patterns or proportional circles.
✓
4. Legend or key
Explain exactly what every color, symbol, line or pattern means.
✓
5. Geographic labels
Label relevant municipalities, cities, rivers, protected areas or other features.
✓
6. Orientation
Include a north arrow or compass.
✓
7. Sources
List the sources you used and the year of the data, when available.
✓
8. Map interpretation
Write three sentences explaining what the map reveals.
⭐ Extension: add a scale bar (optional).
The Legend Part A
Three ways to build a legend
Method 1
Color categories
Best for density, temperature, rainfall, crops.
low density
moderate density
high density
Define the categories with real data.
Method 2
Symbols
Best for minerals, cities, communities, parks.
◆ documented gold mine
● major city
▲ protected area
One symbol = one type of feature.
Method 3
Different sizes
Best for comparing quantities.
● smaller population
● medium population
● larger population
Define sizes, or use real figures.
🤝 You can combine methods if you have a clear reason.

Your Goal Part B

Add a second related data layer or comparison. Explain how they relate.
Example: add major cities and discuss how the two layers relate.
Research Rules Part B
Research rules
1
Use at least two reliable sources – one official or authoritative.
2
Write the source and the data year.
3
Never invent statistics or claim a place has a resource or community without evidence.
4
Use the same units and comparable categories.
5
No exact data? Choose a different theme or say what the limits are.
🔎 Starting points: DANE (population) · IDEAM (climate, environment) · Agencia Nacional de Minería (mining) · official government agencies (Indigenous affairs and land)

Map Interpretation Part B
Three sentences: what does your map reveal?
1What does your map show?
Our map shows ______ in ______.
2What pattern or difference did you discover?
______ is higher/lower/more/fewer in ______ than in ______.
3Why might this information be important?
This pattern can affect ______ because ______.

Example (a population map): The map shows population density in ______. The population is concentrated in a few urban areas, while other areas have lower density. This pattern can affect where roads, schools and hospitals are built.

📝 Your conclusions must be supported by your data.
