# Impact 4 — Unit 1: Pushing the Limits
**Rochester School, Chía, Colombia | 13-year-olds | 45–50 min classes | 6 sessions (1.1–1.6)**

General notes for every session:
- **Choice Theory (Glasser):** every session includes at least one genuine *choice* point (topic, partner, format, or task) and at least one moment built around belonging (pair/group talk), fun (game/movement), or power/competence (visible progress, presenting work). Flagged inline as **[CT]**.
- **Pacing for this age group:** no single activity should run past ~8–10 min. Rotate: listen → talk → move/write → talk. Keep transitions fast and announced ("2 minutes, go").
- **Book pages** are cited so slides can screenshot/recreate the exact exercise.
- Where content overflows 45–50 min, the **[cut if short on time]** tag marks what to drop or send home first.

## Lesson 1.1 — Kickoff + Vocabulary (pp. 8–13)
**Objective:** Students predict the unit theme, learn 12 core vocabulary items about pushing limits, and personalize them.

1. **Title slide** — Unit photo (climber on Dawn Wall, p.8) + title "Pushing the Limits." Hook text: "How far can YOU go?"
2. **Warm-up (5 min):** Show the climber photo only. Pair talk, 90 sec: What risks does he face? Why take them? Cold-call 2–3 pairs.
3. **Quote slide:** *"We have to learn how far we can go." —Cory Richards.* Think-pair-share: what does he mean?
4. **Predict the unit [CT – choice]:** Based on the title, quote, and photo — individually or with a partner (student picks) — predict 2 things this unit will cover.
5. **Personal hook:** "Do you do things that challenge your body or mind? How can pushing your limits change you?" Quick popcorn share (volunteers only — no forced calling; respects autonomy).
6. **Vocab picture set** (surfer at Nazaré, ice climber, desert marathon, p.10–11): "How is each person feeling?" — guess before reading.
7. **New words, round 1** (listen & repeat, audio 1.1–1.2): *pushed himself, limit, obstacle, pain, determination, enables, risks, extreme, physical, mental, opponent, role model, strength, toughest.*
8. **Pair work [CT – choice]:** Each student picks 4 words from the list and gives a personal example ("I push myself when I…"). Model the example bubbles from p.11 first.
9. **Fill-in-the-blank practice** (Cory Richards paragraph, p.12, ex.5) — individual, then pair-check.
10. **New words, round 2** (matching, ex.6): *achieve, boundary, unbelievable, unreachable* → definitions.
11. **[cut if short on time] Your Choice (p.12, ex.7)** — preview only; assign as the take-home/next-class opener: **A)** profile of an athlete, **B)** TV interview role-play, **C)** ad for an extreme sports event.
12. **Exit ticket (3 min):** One sentence using a new word, about yourself. Hand in on paper or say aloud on the way out.

## Lesson 1.2 — Vocabulary → Speaking (pp. 12–13)
**Objective:** Students use new vocabulary to show interest and curiosity in a conversation.

1. **Warm-up game (5 min):** Quick vocab review — teacher says a definition, students shout/write the word (mini whiteboards or just call out). High energy, gets the wiggles out.
2. **Finish Your Choice (p.12, ex.7)** if not done last class — 10 min sharing in small groups **[CT – choice + belonging]**.
3. **Speaking box intro (p.13):** "Showing interest in a conversation" — statement + response phrases (*Wow! What's that like? / No way, I never knew that... / Tell me more about...*).
4. **Listen & notice (audio 1.6):** Students write the phrases they hear.
5. **Listen & respond (audio 1.7):** Students write an appropriate interested response to 5 prompts.
6. **Conversation tip callout:** "Demonstrating curiosity" — ask questions about both the person and the topic.
7. **Spin the wheel game (ex.3, p.13) [CT – fun + choice of topic via spinner]:** Pairs spin a topic wheel; Student A makes a statement on the topic, Student B responds using the interest phrases. Switch roles. Use a real spinner (physical or online wheel) for energy.
8. **Group storytelling (ex.4):** "Two truths and a lie" about a risk you've taken — the group guesses which story is true, using the interest phrases to keep the conversation going. **[CT – belonging + fun]**
9. **Wrap-up:** Cold-call 2 students to share their favorite phrase they used today.

## Lesson 1.3 — Grammar: Embedded Clauses, Questions & Commands (pp. 14–15)
**Objective:** Students form embedded clauses/questions/commands (*I think… / I wonder… / Do you know if… / I'm asking you to…*) correctly, including word-order changes.

1. **Warm-up (5 min):** Write on the board: "Can we climb that mountain in winter?" Ask: how would you ask this *politely* inside another sentence? Elicit "Do you know if we can climb that mountain in winter?" — surface the target structure inductively before naming it.
2. **Grammar box (p.14):** Present the 4 patterns side by side (statement/question/embedded question/embedded command) with the direct vs. embedded pairs. Highlight the word-order shift in "Do you know if…" and the added "to" in commands.
3. **Guided practice (ex.1, listening, audio 1.9):** Individual — complete 5 embedded clauses/questions/commands.
4. **Pair matching-and-building (ex.2):** Match sentence halves, then build 5 original sentences combining the stems (I wonder / I think / I'm asking / Do you remember if) with the given facts about Cory.
5. **New vocab mini-set (p.15, ex.3):** *demonstrate, teamwork, event, struggle, champions* — via the FIRST Robotics reading paragraph. Read aloud together or in pairs.
6. **Applied writing (ex.4):** Pairs write 5 sentences using the given word pairs (wonder/event/time, etc.), each containing an embedded clause/question/command. Model the example given.
7. **Group discussion (ex.5) [CT – belonging]:** Groups brainstorm other "mental challenge" competitions they know, using embedded structures as much as possible ("I wonder if...", "Do you know how...").
8. **Wrap-up (3 min):** One group shares one sentence on the board; class checks word order together.

## Lesson 1.4 — Reading: "No Limits" — Amy Purdy (pp. 16–17)
**Objective:** Students read for gist and detail, and practice the *summarize* strategy.

1. **Warm-up (5 min):** Show only the snowboarding photo + headline "No Limits." Pair prediction: what do you think this story is about?
2. **Pre-teach vocab (ex.2):** *beyond, consequence, in control, optimistic* — guess meaning from context first, dictionary to check, then listen & repeat.
3. **Strategy mini-lesson: Summarize.** Explain: pause after each paragraph and say what it was about *in your own words* — not a re-read, a re-tell.
4. **Guided read, paragraph 1–2 (audio 1.13):** Model summarizing paragraph 1 aloud yourself first ("So far: Amy is a world-class athlete who lost both legs but didn't let it stop her").
5. **Paired read + summarize (ex.3–4):** Students read the rest in pairs, pausing to summarize each paragraph to their partner without looking at the text.
6. **Comprehension check (ex.5, groups of 3–4):** 6 questions — how she lost her legs, consequences, learning to walk again, other successes, how she helps others, character traits. **[CT – belonging]**
7. **Discussion (ex.6):** Positive/negative impacts of her illness; "disadvantages can become advantages" — do you agree, with your own example? What does "your spirit doesn't break" mean to you?
8. **[cut if short on time]** If pressed for time, assign ex.6 discussion questions as a written reflection for homework instead of in-class discussion.
9. **Wrap-up:** One-word check-out — each student says one word that describes Amy Purdy.

## Lesson 1.5 — Video (Cory Richards) + Grammar: Adding Emphasis (pp. 18–20)
**Objective:** Students discuss a video using prior knowledge, then learn cleft-sentence emphasis structures (*The reason why… is that… / The thing that… is…*).

1. **Warm-up discussion (ex.1, p.18, 5 min) [CT – choice of question to answer]:** In pairs, pick 2 of the 3 prompts: uncomfortable/dangerous situation you experienced; what you do when stressed; a photo that taught you something.
2. **Predict (ex.2):** Title is *"A Tribute to Discomfort."* Using title + photo + prior knowledge of Cory, predict the video's content.
3. **Watch video 1.1** — preview the ex.4 questions first so students watch with purpose; take notes.
4. **Pair discussion (ex.4):** 4 comprehension questions about adventure, risk-taking, storytelling, and how his photography changed.
5. **[cut if short on time] Observation activity (ex.5):** Pause to closely observe a classmate or object in the room, noticing something new — connects to Cory's idea that "education comes from observing." Good candidate to cut/shorten if time is tight.
6. **Group discussion (ex.6) [CT – belonging]:** "Richness of struggle" — share a time something difficult was actually good for you.
7. **Transition to grammar:** On the board, write "I won't go surfing. I don't like swimming in the ocean." Ask: how can we combine these to *emphasize* the reason? Elicit toward "The reason why I won't go surfing is that I don't like swimming in the ocean."
8. **Grammar box (p.20):** Present "The place (where)... is..." / "The thing (that)... is that..." / "The reason (why)... is that..." patterns.
9. **Guided practice (ex.1):** Individually rewrite 5 sentences to add emphasis.
10. **Card game (ex.2) [CT – fun + choice]:** Pairs draw sport cards and build emphasis sentences about them ("The thing that Carolina really loves is practicing yoga").
11. **Wrap-up:** Each pair says one emphasis sentence about themselves out loud before leaving.

*Note: this session is content-heavy (video + full grammar point). If your class runs slower, split video (steps 1–6) and grammar (steps 7–11) across two days and move Writing (1.6 below) later — better than rushing either.*

## Lesson 1.6 — Writing: Biography + Mission (pp. 21–23)
**Objective:** Students research and write a short biography using time-connector language, then apply the unit theme in a choice-based mission project.

1. **Warm-up (5 min):** Show the Khalida Popal photo only. Guess: who is she, what might her story be?
2. **Genre box (p.21):** "A biography tells the story of a person's life." Introduce connector phrases: *afterwards, at first, eventually, later on, more recently, ultimately.*
3. **Model text (ex.1):** Pairs read Khalida Popal's biography and underline the organizing words/phrases they used.
4. **Plan (ex.2) [CT – choice]:** Students choose an athlete (real, and ideally one they're genuinely interested in) and note 3 major life events.
5. **Write (ex.3):** Draft a short biography using at least 3 of the connector phrases.
6. **Peer review (ex.4):** Swap with a partner; use the checklist (background given / connector words used / recent accomplishments included / strong conclusion) and give feedback. **[CT – belonging]**
7. **Transition to Mission:** Show the "Test Your Limits" quote: *"Adventure is anything that puts us outside our comfort zone." —Cory Richards.*
8. **Discuss (ex.1, p.22):** How does Cory test his limits as a photojournalist?
9. **Watch video 1.2**, taking notes on Cory's concerns and positive thoughts; complete the 4 sentence starters (ex.3).
10. **Your Choice mission project (ex. "Make an Impact," p.23) [CT – strong choice + power/competence]:** Students pick ONE:
   - **A. Wellness video** — demo a limit-pushing activity (yoga, dance, martial arts) and its benefits.
   - **B. Podcast** — pair interview script about an athlete who overcame a personal/social struggle.
   - **C. Poster** — design a school club offering an academic challenge (e.g., robotics), with goals and events.
11. **Work time + share:** Give remaining time for project work in chosen groups; if time is short, this becomes the unit's take-home project, due next class, with 5–10 min of class time reserved for presenting/displaying finished work.

**[Not scheduled — optional extension / homework bank]:** *Literacy Builder: "Josie Takes a Risk"* (pp. 24–25, realistic fiction, "create closure" strategy) did not fit in 6 sessions. Recommended use: assign as a homework reading + written closure (ex.6, choice of A/B/C) once Unit 1 wraps, or swap in for the Mission project block above if your group finishes early.
